Worked examples

10 × 10 ft slab at 4 inches: about 1.23 cubic yards including 10% waste — 56 bags of 80 lb, 74 bags of 60 lb, or 89 bags of 50 lb mix.

16 × 30 ft slab at 4 inches: 160 cu ft ÷ 27 = 5.93 yd³ exact, 6.52 yd³ to order with 10% waste — 267 bags of 80 lb (or 356 of 60 lb) if bag-mixed.

Ready-mix truck vs. bags

As a contractor rule of thumb, projects over ~1.5 to 2 cubic yards (60–90 eighty-pound bags) are significantly easier, faster and more cost-effective with a ready-mix truck. Hand-mixing 90+ bags requires heavy labor and risks cold joints. A standard transit mixer carries 10 yd³; plan multiple trucks above that. An estimation error of just 1/2 inch in slab thickness can cause an 11% volume shortfall, leading to short-load surcharges ($120–$250) or cold joints — which is why the waste buffer matters.

Professional safety: for load-bearing foundations, retaining walls over 4 feet, or structural columns, always consult a licensed structural engineer and local building codes. This page gives material volumetric math, not certified structural engineering.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how many cubic yards of concrete I need?

Multiply length (ft) × width (ft) × (thickness (in) ÷ 12), divide the cubic feet by 27, then add 10% for waste and uneven subgrade.

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 10×10 slab?

A 10×10 ft slab at 4 inches needs about 1.23 cubic yards including 10% waste: 56 bags of 80 lb, 74 bags of 60 lb, or 89 bags of 50 lb mix.

How much extra concrete waste should I order?

Order 10% extra for standard slabs, driveways and footings. For rough, hand-dug post holes or irregular excavations, increase waste contingency to 15%. Ordering exact theoretical volume almost always leaves you short.

When should I order a ready-mix truck instead of bags?

Above ~1.5 to 2 cubic yards (60–90 eighty-pound bags), a ready-mix truck is usually cheaper per yard, faster, and avoids cold joints.

How thick should a residential concrete slab be?

Walkways 3.5–4 in, patios 4 in, passenger-car driveways 4–5 in, heavy-truck driveways and garage slabs 5–6 in with rebar.

What if my order is under 4 cubic yards?

Expect a short-load delivery fee — combine small pours when possible, or bag-mix very small jobs.
